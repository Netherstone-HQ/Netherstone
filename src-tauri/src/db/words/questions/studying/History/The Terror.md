# The Terror

From September 1793 to July 1794, the revolutionary government used mass arrests and executions against anyone it saw as an enemy. It sits in the radical phase of [The French Revolution](The%20French%20Revolution.md).

## Key points

- **Why it happened.** France was at war abroad and facing revolts at home, like the Vendée. The Jacobins argued that the republic could only survive by crushing its enemies
- **Who ran it.** The Committee of Public Safety, with Maximilien Robespierre its best-known member
- **How.** The Law of Suspects (September 1793) allowed arrest on vague suspicion. The Revolutionary Tribunal tried cases quickly, often with no real defence
- **Scale.** About 17,000 people were officially executed, and many more died in prison or without trial
- **Victims.** Marie Antoinette (October 1793), rival revolutionaries like Danton (April 1794), and thousands of ordinary people
- **End.** In July 1794 (Thermidor in the revolutionary calendar), the Convention turned on Robespierre, who was executed the next day
