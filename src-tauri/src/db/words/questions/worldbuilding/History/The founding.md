# The founding

About two hundred years before the story starts, a drought pushed families out of the inland valleys toward the sea. Most went south. A few, led by Ansel Vell, went north to a coast everyone else called worthless.

## What they found

The northern shore was cold, flat and grey, with shallow bays that flooded at spring tide and dried in summer. Ansel saw what the others missed: every dry bay left a crust of salt. Salt meant preserved fish, and preserved fish meant trade with the inland towns all winter.

## The first years

- The families cut pans into the flats at the mouth of a sheltered bay, the start of [Harrowgate](../Places/Harrowgate.md)
- The first winter killed a third of them. The survivors buried their dead on the headland that later became [Wrecker's Point](../Places/Wrecker%27s%20Point.md)
- By the tenth year, salt carts were going inland every month, and the Vells held the only pans worth owning

## What it left behind

The founding is why the Vells still run Harrowgate, why the headland is holy and why people say it's haunted, and why coast people distrust anyone from the inland valleys. Most of the conflicts in [The Vell family](../People/The%20Vell%20family.md) go back to promises Ansel made that first winter.
