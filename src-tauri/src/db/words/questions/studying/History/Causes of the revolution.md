# Causes of the revolution

Historians group the causes into long-term problems and short-term triggers. A good answer links them: the long-term problems made France fragile, the triggers made it break in 1789. What followed is in [The French Revolution](The%20French%20Revolution.md).

## Long-term

- **An unfair society.** France was split into three estates: clergy, nobles and everyone else. The first two paid little or no tax; the Third Estate, about 97% of people, carried the burden
- **Debt.** Wars, especially support for the American War of Independence, left the crown deeply in debt
- **New ideas.** Enlightenment writers like Rousseau and Montesquieu questioned the divine right of kings, and the American revolution showed it could be done

## Short-term

- **Bankruptcy.** By 1788 the crown could not borrow more, and the nobles blocked tax reform
- **Bread.** The harvest of 1788 failed and bread prices soared in 1789, bringing hungry crowds onto the streets
- **The Estates-General.** Louis XVI called it to raise taxes. Voting by estate meant the first two could always outvote the Third, so the Third Estate broke away as the National Assembly
- **A weak king.** Louis XVI hesitated between reform and force, and lost the trust of both sides
