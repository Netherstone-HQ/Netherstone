# Languages

Most people on [The Salt Coast](Places/The%20Salt%20Coast.md) speak Coast, a trade tongue with old inland words worn down by the sea. Names follow a few habits, so new ones sound like they belong.

## How names are built

- Places end in *-gate* (a harbour mouth), *-holm* (an island) or *-mere* (a salt pan)
- Family names come from a trade or a landmark: Vell (a salt rake), Corran (a cliff path)
- Given names are short, one or two sounds: Mara, Tobin, Ansel, Wren

## Words

| Word | Meaning | Used like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vell | the wooden rake used on the salt pans | "She was born with a vell in her hand" |
| greywater | the brine left after the first drying | a sour person is "full of greywater" |
| lampright | the duty of keeping a coast light burning | "Tobin holds the lampright" |
| harrow | to drag a net or a pan clean | gave [Harrowgate](Places/Harrowgate.md) its name |
| drowned coin | anything taken from a wreck | not spoken of in polite company |

## Old Inland

The founders spoke Old Inland, now used only in oaths and on gravestones. The Vells still swear their heads of house in it. See [The Vell family](People/The%20Vell%20family.md).
