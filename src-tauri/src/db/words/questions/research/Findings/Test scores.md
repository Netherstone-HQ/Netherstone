# Test scores

There's no reliable evidence yet that handwriting notes leads to better test scores than typing them.

## Evidence

- The original study found handwriting helped on conceptual questions: [Mueller and Oppenheimer 2014](../Sources/Mueller%20and%20Oppenheimer%202014.md)
- A replication with an extra digital pen condition found few reliable differences: [Morehead et al 2019](../Sources/Morehead%20et%20al%202019.md)
- A direct replication with a larger sample found no reliable difference, and pooled studies put the effect close to zero: [Urry et al 2021](../Sources/Urry%20et%20al%202021.md)

## How sure

Moderately. The later studies are larger and closer copies of the original, so they carry more weight. But all three test learning shortly after a single short talk, which is not how students study for a real course.

## So what

A ban on laptops in class isn't supported by these studies on learning grounds alone. Whether notes are typed or written seems to matter less than what the student does with them afterwards. See [Open questions](../Open%20questions.md).
