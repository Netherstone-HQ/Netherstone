# Urry et al 2021

Urry, H. L., et al. (2021). Don't ditch the laptop just yet: A direct replication of Mueller and Oppenheimer's (2014) study 1 plus mini meta-analyses across similar studies. *Psychological Science*, 32(3), 326–339.

## Claim

Laptop notes are longer and more verbatim, as the original found, but that doesn't translate into a reliable difference in what students learn.

## Method

A direct replication of the first experiment in [Mueller and Oppenheimer 2014](Mueller%20and%20Oppenheimer%202014.md), run by a group of researchers and students with a larger sample, followed by small meta-analyses pooling similar studies.

## Results

- Laptop users again wrote more words and copied more verbatim
- No reliable difference between the groups on factual or conceptual questions
- Pooled across studies, the effect on test performance was uncertain and could be close to zero

## What I take from it

The strongest evidence so far, because it repeats the original method closely and pools other studies. It separates the two parts of the original claim: how notes differ (holds up) and what that does to learning (doesn't clearly hold up).
